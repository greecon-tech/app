The enclosure is the one box on the wall that holds the kit: the fuses and the 24 V supply on its mains side; the converter, the modules and the terminal blocks on its low-voltage side; and the box itself, if there is room. It keeps dust and water out, it keeps mains and low voltage apart, and, labelled properly, it lets the next person understand the kit without asking anyone.

## How it qualifies

Any make qualifies that meets every line below.

**It qualifies if:**

- {{cb}} It is rated **IP54** indoors, and **IP65** outdoors or where it can get wet.
- {{cb}} **It is large enough.** Lay the kit's parts side by side on a desk and measure: the DIN rail takes them all, with room for the terminal blocks and for air around the supply.
- {{cb}} It has a DIN rail, terminal blocks for the data cable and for 24 V, and fuses or small breakers: one for the supply's mains side and, where the electrician places them here, those of the meters' voltage taps.
- {{cb}} It has a cable gland for every cable that enters, at the bottom.
- {{cb}} Its door closes with a key or a tool.
- {{cb}} At a site with a 4G router: it is plastic, or the router's aerial can be led outside. A steel enclosure weakens the signal.

::: stop
**Mains and low voltage are kept apart in it, and the mains side is the electrician's.** The project manager mounts the enclosure and connects the low-voltage side, and never connects, opens or alters the mains side.
:::

## What is labelled

| What | Its label |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Each device | Its unit number, as set at the office |
| The converter | Its settings: gateway mode, 9600 baud, 8 data bits, no parity, 1 stop bit, port 502, and its address |
| Every wire | A number, at both ends |
| The UPS | The date of its battery |
| Inside the lid | The site name and the date |

**Never write a code or a password on the box, the enclosure or a label.**

## Fitting

{{who:pm}} mounts the enclosure and the low-voltage parts in it. {{who:e}} connects the mains side.

- {{cb}} **The place:** a dry wall, away from heat, where the door can be opened fully and the labels read. Outdoors, out of the sun where that is possible.
- {{cb}} **Cables come in at the bottom,** each through its own gland. Unused holes are plugged.
- {{cb}} **Mains on one side, low voltage on the other,** as the drawing shows, and the data cable away from power cables.
- {{cb}} The box goes inside if there is room, otherwise on a shelf beside it.
- {{cb}} **Photograph the inside before closing it,** with every label readable.

## In the platform

The enclosure is not in the platform. What the platform keeps is what is written on its labels: each device's unit number and the converter's address, on the site's install card.

## What goes wrong

| What you see | Likely cause | What to do |
|-------------------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| **Water or dust inside** | A gland that was not tightened, an unused hole, or IP54 where IP65 was needed | Tighten and plug. Outdoors, or where it is hosed down, change it for IP65 |
| **The box restarts on hot days** | The box and the supply in a small closed enclosure, in the sun or beside a heat source | Move the box to a shelf beside the enclosure, or move the enclosure into the shade |
| **Nobody can tell which wire is which** | The numbers were left off | Number both ends now, against the photograph, and take a new photograph |
| **The 4G router hears nothing with the door shut** | A steel enclosure | Lead the aerial outside, as the router's manual allows ([sheet HW04](#hw-cell)) |
