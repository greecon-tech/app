# Launch kit

Everything needed to put Greecon on the market with a project manager, written for people who are not engineers.
Each page says what to do, in what order, and what you should see when it worked.

| Read | If you are | It covers |
|---|---|---|
| [01 Founder go-live](01-founder-go-live.md) | The founder, once | Putting the platform on the internet: Railway, the two addresses, the first sign-in, email and text messages, the demo, the outside checks, backups, and a checklist to tick |
| [02 Project manager manual](02-project-manager-manual.md) | The project manager, always | A customer from first call to day 90: survey, onboarding, preparing the box, install day, the setup list step by step, the four kinds of site, commissioning checks, handover, aftercare, and a rehearsal on a desk |
| [03 Hardware and wiring](03-hardware-and-wiring.md) | The project manager and the electrician | What to buy for each kind of site (by specification, with planning prices), the box, the converter, the meters, the water sensors, the network, the power, and the wiring |
| [04 Sales kit](04-sales-kit.md) | The founder and the project manager | The offer and its price, who to call, the questions, the pitch and a ten-minute demo, what may and may not be said, objections, the pilot order form (a draft for a lawyer), follow-up, and emails |
| [05 Runbooks](05-runbooks.md) | The founder and the project manager | The morning check, and what to do when a box is offline, a reading is wrong, a message did not arrive, the platform is down, a release is bad, a person is locked out, data is asked for or deleted, there is a security incident, and for backups |

**The decisions behind all of this** (who the first customers are, what is sold and at what price, who does what, what
is deliberately left for later) are in [27 Launch decisions](../27-launch-decisions.md). Every one can be changed:
that page says how, and what would make you.

## The order of things

1. **The founder** works through [01](01-founder-go-live.md) (half a day) and ticks its checklist.
2. **The project manager** reads [02](02-project-manager-manual.md) and [03](03-hardware-and-wiring.md), buys one kit,
   and does the **rehearsal** ([02, section 13](02-project-manager-manual.md#13-rehearsal-the-whole-job-on-a-desk)) on a desk.
3. **The founder** gets the order form read by a lawyer, settles who pays for hardware during the pilot, and
   gets quotes from an electrician ([04, last section](04-sales-kit.md#11-before-the-first-signature)).
4. **Both** start the conversations ([04](04-sales-kit.md)). **Five paid pilots** is the aim; **twenty
   conversations** is how it is learned whether that will happen.
5. Every morning: the check in [05](05-runbooks.md).

## What these pages are not

They are desk research and engineering judgement, tested against the software here, not the opinion of a lawyer,
an electrical engineer or a process engineer. Where a page says **a lawyer** or **an electrician** must look at
something, that is a requirement and not a suggestion. The prices are planning figures until real quotes replace them.
